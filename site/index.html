Working with the material

Thickness & weight

Slabs are 2 cm as standard; 3 cm on request for worktops and heavy traffic.

Allow roughly 54 kg per m² at 2 cm for marble and around 48 kg per m² for travertine. Confirm dead loads with your engineer before cladding.

Bathrooms

Marble and travertine both work in wet areas — the issue is not water but what is used to clean them.

Seal before grouting, keep descalers and acidic cleaners away, and expect a honed finish to hide water marks better than a polished one.

Facades

Travertine is the traditional choice and handles the Gulf climate well. Use filled slabs outdoors, and a mechanical fixing system rather than adhesive alone.

Darker stone gains a lot of heat in direct sun — worth thinking about on a west elevation.

Stairs & floors

Polished stone is beautiful and slippery. On stairs, around pools and in entrances we recommend honed, brushed or leathered finishes, or a bush-hammered nosing.

Ask for the slip rating if the project has to satisfy a specification.

Sealing

Use an impregnating (penetrating) sealer, not a surface coating — it lets the stone breathe. Apply after installation and before grouting.

Re-apply on kitchen tops roughly once a year, on floors every two to three.

What to avoid

Marble, travertine, limestone and onyx are calcium based, so acid etches them: lemon, vinegar, wine, descaler and most bathroom sprays leave dull marks.

Clean with a pH-neutral product. Quartzite is far more resistant if a client insists on a hard-working surface.

General guidance, not a specification. For a particular stone in a particular position, ask us — we will tell you what we would and would not do.
